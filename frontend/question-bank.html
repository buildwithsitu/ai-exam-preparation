Question 1: Inorder Traversal & Min-Heap Properties

Which of the following statements is strictly TRUE regarding Binary Search Trees (BST) and Binary Heaps?

  1. The Inorder traversal of any valid Binary Search Tree always produces values in strictly sorted (ascending) order.
  2. In a Min-Heap, the second smallest element must always reside at the right child of the root.
  3. Finding the maximum element in a Min-Heap of n nodes takes worst-case O(1) time.
  4. A Min-Heap is always a strictly balanced Binary Search Tree.
Click to View Correct Option & Detailed Explanation

Correct Answer: (A) The Inorder traversal of any valid Binary Search Tree always produces values in strictly sorted order.

Conceptual Breakdown:

  • Why A is TRUE: By mathematical definition, for every node X in a BST, all keys in the left subtree are smaller than X, and all keys in the right subtree are greater than X. An Inorder traversal visits Left → Root → Right, which strictly guarantees non-decreasing sorted order.
  • Why B is FALSE: In a Min-Heap, the second smallest element can be at either the left child (index 1) OR the right child (index 2) of the root node.
  • Why C is FALSE: In a Min-Heap, the maximum element resides in one of the leaf nodes. There are ⌈n/2⌉ leaves, so finding the maximum requires a linear scan: O(n) time, not O(1).
  • Why D is FALSE: A Heap is a complete binary tree satisfying the heap-order property, whereas a BST satisfies the binary-search-order property. They are entirely different tree data structures.

Question 2: Eigenvalues and Trace of a Matrix

Let M be a 2×2 real matrix whose determinant is 12 and whose trace is 7. What are the eigenvalues (λ1, λ2) of matrix M?

  1. λ1 = 2, λ2 = 6
  2. λ1 = 3, λ2 = 4
  3. λ1 = 1, λ2 = 12
  4. λ1 = −3, λ2 = −4
Click to View Correct Option & Detailed Explanation

Correct Answer: (B) λ1 = 3, λ2 = 4

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation:

  1. Trace Property: The trace of any square matrix equals the sum of its eigenvalues:
    Trace(M) = λ1 + λ2 = 7
  2. Determinant Property: The determinant of a square matrix equals the product of its eigenvalues:
    Det(M) = λ1 × λ2 = 12
  3. Characteristic Equation:
    λ2 − (Trace)λ + Det = 0
    λ2 − 7λ + 12 = 0
  4. Factoring:
    (λ − 3)(λ − 4) = 0 ⇒ λ1 = 3, λ2 = 4.

Question 3: Successive Discounts Calculation

A merchant marks a textbook at ₹ 500. He offers two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What is the final selling price of the book?

  1. ₹ 350
  2. ₹ 360
  3. ₹ 375
  4. ₹ 340
Click to View Correct Option & Detailed Explanation

Correct Answer: (B) ₹ 360

Short-Cut Formula & Step-by-Step Method:

Method 1 (Equivalent Discount Formula):

Net Discount = (d1 + d2) − (d1 × d2 / 100)
Net Discount = (20 + 10) − (20 × 10 / 100) = 30 − 2 = 28%

Selling Price = Marked Price × (100 − 28)%
Selling Price = 500 × 0.72 = ₹ 360.

Question 4: Shortest Job First (SJF) and Starvation

Which of the following CPU scheduling algorithms is susceptible to process starvation (indefinite blocking of longer burst processes)?

  1. First-Come, First-Served (FCFS)
  2. Shortest Job First (SJF / Non-preemptive)
  3. Round Robin with an optimal time quantum
  4. First-In, First-Out (FIFO)
Click to View Correct Option & Detailed Explanation

Correct Answer: (B) Shortest Job First (SJF / Non-preemptive)

Conceptual Breakdown:

In Shortest Job First (SJF), the CPU scheduler selects the process with the smallest anticipated CPU burst time. If shorter processes arrive continuously in the ready queue, longer processes may be indefinitely postponed. This condition is termed Starvation.

Mitigation: Modern operating systems use the Aging technique, gradually increasing the priority of processes as they wait.
